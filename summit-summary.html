Summit Intro & Mindset

Brandon

The summit opens with goal-setting and framing digital advertising as a poker table where "you can count cards and the dealer wants you to win." The emphasis is on giving yourself both a selfish and an unselfish reason to succeed.

Two reasons to win — set a selfish goal (income, lifestyle) and an unselfish one (family, team, community). The unselfish reason is what keeps you going when things get hard.
Customer journey narrative — from "Funnel Daydreamer" (thinking about ads) to having a full system running. The summit maps each step of this journey.
Digital advertising = gambling where the dealer wants you to win — Meta and Google profit when your ads succeed, so the platforms are incentivised to help you. Learn their rules and the odds tilt in your favour.
Ads are art vs science — frameworks give you structure, but there are no hard and fast rules. Everything works to some degree; the job is finding what works best for your niche.

Customer Avatar & Green Brain

Brandon

"Nobody knows the ideal customer better than the business owner." The customer avatar goes beyond demographics into what makes someone move — their fears, desires, and the language they use to describe both.

Avatar Speedrun

Away From & Towards — map what your customer is running from (pain, fear, frustration) and running towards (outcome, aspiration, relief). Like Tom & Jerry: the cat pushes and the cheese pulls.
Tribalism — people identify with groups. Position your offer as belonging to a tribe they want to join, and against the tribe they want to leave.

Red Brain vs Green Brain

Red Brain (feature-focused) — "How to do Facebook Ads," "10 tips for better SEO." Logical, process-oriented. This is what most marketers default to.
Green Brain (benefit-focused) — "How to never rely on word of mouth again," "Stop losing customers to your competitors." Emotional, outcome-oriented. This is what stops the scroll.
Green Brain Roadmap: What it is → The result it delivers → The emotional effect of that result. Write Green Brain headlines with Red Brain sub-headlines underneath.

Ads: Content Creation

Tiarna

The number one rule of ad content: don't make your ad look like an ad. No logos, no brand intros, no "Hey, I'm [Name] from [Company]." People scroll past ads — they stop for content that speaks to them.

Camouflaging Ads

Lead with "what's in it for me" — the viewer doesn't care about your brand yet. They care about their problem. Open with the problem or the promise.
Images are the exception — image ads are absorbed instantly. They work differently from video; treat them as a separate format, not a lesser one.
Silent ads can work — a 48-second video of Brandon sitting in silence playing with a microphone generated 12 leads at $16.48/lead. Pattern interrupts outperform polished content.

Writing Video Scripts

3-step framework: (1) Stop the right person scrolling, (2) Prove your point, (3) Tell them what to do next.
Competitor research — use Meta Ad Library to steal inspiration. Search competitors, see what they're running, adapt their angles for your niche.

Testimonial Framework 2.0

8-step interview sequence: (1) Introduce yourself, (2) Before/problem state, (3) Hesitations & reasons for buying, (4) What changed/the solution, (5) What they loved most, (6) After state, (7) Personal/professional effect, (8) "If anyone watching is unsure about booking a call, what would you tell them?"
Testimonial VSL variant — instead of you selling on camera, record an interview with your best customer following this framework. Let the customer do the convincing.

Image vs Video

52% of marketers use video, and 60% agree video gets more engagement — but image ads are easier, cheaper, and can be optimised. Carousel ads combine the simplicity of images with the eye-catching nature of video.

Copywriting: Meta & Google

Brandon

"Your ad sells the click, not the product." The ad's only job is to get someone to the landing page. Don't try to close the sale in the ad copy.

Meta Ads Structure

Three interchangeable frameworks: Hook/Story/Offer, Hook/Body/CTA, or Emotional/Logical/Emotional. All follow the same rhythm: grab, prove, direct.
Rule of One — one idea, one promise, one story, one emotion, one CTA. Multiple messages compete with each other and dilute everything.

Writing Hooks

Good hooks imply who it's for — "Attention Perth homeowners" is explicit. "Sick of dodgy tradies?" is implied. Implied hooks outperform because they feel personal, not broadcast.
Imply who it's NOT for — exclusion creates curiosity. "This isn't for businesses that are already booked out" makes everyone else lean in.
Pique curiosity — the hook should open a loop the reader needs to close. Don't give the answer; promise one.

Body Copy Proof Tools

6 tools: Urgency (limited time), Scarcity (limited supply), Social Proof (testimonials, numbers), Authority (credentials, media), Familiar Language (mirror their words), Benefits over features.

CTAs & Headlines

CTA formula: Action + emotional benefit. "Book your free quote and stop worrying about dodgy tradies" beats "Click here to learn more."
Meta headlines: 28-character limit, green brain language, pique curiosity. The headline appears below the image — it's the last thing before the click.

Google Ads Copywriting

Headline 1 (x2-4): Your service name — "Garage Door Installation," "Quality Garage Door Install."
Headline 2 (x2-4): Shortened hook/motivator — "Skip The Renovation Disaster," "No Dodgy Technicians," "Hassle-Free Service."
Headline 3 (x2-4): Shortened CTA — "Get a Free Quote," "Generate Your Free Quote."
Description 1: Shortened hook (towards or away from motivator), max 90 chars.
Description 2: Simplified body — justify why your company is the best, max 90 chars.
Short-form Meta framework: Headline 1 = Your service (x2-4), Headline 2 = Stop them with your point (x2-4), Headline 3 = CTA (x2-4), Description 1 = Expand on your point (x1-2), Description 2 = Justify your point (x1-2).

Meta Andromeda & Creative Diversity

Brandon / Patsy

Meta's Andromeda algorithm update introduced a retrieval layer before the ad auction. It now rewards creative diversity — the system evaluates each ad independently, so more unique creatives means more chances to win auctions at lower cost.

10-15 unique ads per ad set (minimum) — "unique" means unique reasons, unique transcript start to finish, and completely unique ad copy. Duplicating and changing the first sentence no longer works.
Curious vs Committed language — ad copy determines lead quality. "How my clients grow their revenue" (committed) attracts hotter leads than "How businesses grow" (curious). Choose based on your sales capacity.
Negativity bias — people are naturally skeptical. Frame messaging so readers "disagree in the right direction." Use doubt constructively to pull attention.
Budget concentrates naturally — most spend will flow to 1-2 winners. Keep all ads running; Meta needs the variety to find winners. Never judge an ad set by its weakest performer.

Creative Diversity Roadmap

Step 1: Audit — review existing ads. How many truly unique concepts do you have? Most accounts have 2-3 ideas repeated with slight variations.
Step 2: Segment into messaging pillars — group your value props into distinct themes (e.g., speed, quality, trust, price, results). Each pillar should feel like a different conversation.
Step 3: 6-10 unique concepts per pillar — each concept has its own angle, script, visual approach, and copy. This is where most people underinvest.
What no longer works: Duplicating your ad and changing the first sentence slightly. Duplicating the video and changing the first 3 seconds. Running only 2 or 3 ads.

Conversions & Funnels

Brandon

The full pipeline is Clicks → Conversions → Close. Most people obsess over clicks and ignore the conversion layer — the part where a visitor becomes a lead and a lead becomes a booking.

Two Types of Resistance

Physical resistance — every click, keystroke, and scroll is friction. Reduce steps ruthlessly. Amazon's "one-click buy" patent existed because Bezos understood this.
Psychological resistance — trust, purchase intent, and cognitive load. The landing page's job is to lower all three.

Funnel Types

One-step funnel (e.g., form → call): Speed is everything. Call the lead within 5 minutes. Automate the first communication. The longer you wait, the colder they get.
Two-step funnel (e.g., form → booking → call): Limit booking availability to the next 3 days. Wider windows create procrastination and no-shows.

Attendance & Booking Rules

Book today/tomorrow/day-after only — urgency drives attendance. Letting someone book 2 weeks out guarantees they'll forget.
15-minute slots — shorter slots feel less committal. The actual call can run longer, but the booking psychology matters.
Reminder cadence: Booking confirmation → 24hrs before → 1hr before → 30min before. Each touchpoint reduces no-shows.

Conversion Tracking

Events to track: One-step funnel = Leads. Two-step funnel = Leads + Schedules. Set up when landing pages are done.
It's not perfect (especially post iOS 14). Always refer to your CRM for true conversion data. You can make tracking more accurate but it's expensive and complicated — not worth it under $20K/month ad spend.
Quality vs Quantity seesaw — making things too easy (fewer form fields, broader targeting) increases volume but can hurt lead quality. Find the balance point for your niche.
Microsoft Clarity — free tool (better and faster than HotJar) that records screen sessions of every visitor. Watch recordings to get qualitative feedback on user behaviour.
Speed kills sales: Automate the first communication. If you can't call within 5 minutes, at least send an automated text/email that acknowledges their enquiry and sets expectations.

Landing Page Optimisation

Iszy

People scan, they don't read. Every element on the page either builds momentum toward the CTA or creates friction. The goal is to remove doubt more than increase desire.

Momentum principle — headline catches attention → subheadline builds interest → CTA captures action. Every scroll should move the visitor closer, not sideways.
Remove doubt > increase desire — most visitors already have some interest (they clicked the ad). Your job is to eliminate the reasons they'd leave, not hype them further.
Mobile-first — design for mobile, then adapt for desktop. Most traffic comes from Meta mobile placements.
Consistent branding — the LP should feel like an extension of the ad. Colour, tone, imagery must match or trust breaks immediately.
Reduce text ruthlessly — if a section doesn't directly address an objection or build credibility, cut it. Long pages aren't bad; unfocused pages are.
Split LPs by service/audience — one page per offer per audience. A generic "all services" page converts worse than targeted variants.
FAQs higher up — objection handling belongs mid-page, not buried at the bottom.
Images reduce cognitive load — add relevant images and sections below the VSL. Thank you pages boost show rates.
Websites vs Landing Pages — a website is essentialism gone wrong (too many paths). A landing page is Jiro-style focus: one job, done perfectly.

Neuromarketing & Psychographics

Patsy

Understanding not just who your customer is (demographics) but why they buy. 95% of purchasing decisions are subconscious — messaging must reach the emotional brain before the logical one.

Decision breakdown: 50% emotion, 30% process, 20% logic. Lead with feeling, structure the journey, close with reason.
Towards vs Away motivators — people move towards pleasure or away from pain. Identify which drives your avatar: "I want financial freedom" (towards) vs "I can't keep living paycheck to paycheck" (away).
Customer avatar definition — go beyond age/location. Map their daily frustrations, aspirations, the language they use, and the emotional state they're in when they first encounter your ad.
Emotional messaging framework — write ads that name the feeling, not just the problem. "Tired of being overlooked" hits harder than "Grow your business."
Mirror their language — use the exact words your audience uses in testimonials, reviews, and intake forms. Their vocabulary is your best copywriting tool.

3-Phase Ad Process

Raj

A structured 90-day framework for launching and scaling paid campaigns across Google and Meta, with specific landing page work at each stage.

Phase 1: Data Gathering (Days 0-30)

Launch initial campaigns to collect data. Don't optimise yet — you're buying information.
Meta: Broad targeting, multiple creatives. Let the algorithm learn.
Google: Build keyword lists, set up search campaigns, gather search term reports.
Landing pages: Use a baseline page. Track heatmaps and scroll depth for Phase 2.

Phase 2: Optimisation (Days 30-60)

Cut underperforming ads and keywords. Double down on what's working.
Refine audience targeting based on actual conversion data.
Start A/B testing landing pages using Phase 1 insights.

Phase 3: Scaling (Days 60-90+)

Increase budgets on proven campaigns. Open new ad sets only when current ones plateau.
Expand into lookalike audiences and new platforms.
Continue testing new creatives — Andromeda still rewards diversity at scale.

Custom Audiences & Targeting

Ciara

Custom audiences let you target people who have already interacted with your business. The key is building a data hierarchy — not all interactions are equal.

Data hierarchy (highest to lowest value): Closed sales → Qualified leads → Pixel-tracked bookings → Form submissions → Landing page viewers → Social media engagers.
Segmentation — build separate audiences for each level. Don't lump website visitors with paying customers; they need completely different messaging.
Lookalike audiences — seed them with your highest-quality data (closed sales list) rather than broad website traffic. A 1% lookalike from buyers outperforms a 1% lookalike from page visitors.
Retargeting layers — show different ads to people at different stages. Someone who visited the LP but didn't book needs a different message than someone who booked but didn't show.

Marketing as Debating at Scale

Brandon

Marketing is constructing sufficient reasons for someone to take action. Every person has a worldview (naive realism) — they believe they see the world as it truly is. Your job is to build enough evidence to shift that view.

Naive realism — people believe their perception is objective truth. Marketing must work within their existing beliefs, not against them. Start from where they are, not where you want them to be.
Constructing sufficient reasons — each person needs a different amount of evidence to act. Some need one testimonial; others need 12 touchpoints. Your system must accommodate both.
Lowering psychological resistance — instead of pushing harder, remove barriers. It's easier to make the path smoother than to make the push stronger.
Reddit test — posted anonymously on Reddit to gauge raw, unbiased reactions to messaging. Real-world feedback without brand bias. Useful for testing hooks and value propositions.
Skepticism ads — ads that lean into doubt ("You're probably thinking this won't work for you...") outperform hype-based messaging because they match the reader's internal monologue.
Core principle: The prospect is already having a conversation in their head about your category. Your ad's job is to join that conversation, not start a new one.

Nurture Sequences & Workflows

Team

Three types of nurture sequences, each with a distinct job. The lead doesn't care about your process — they care about their problem. Meet them where they are on the awareness spectrum.

Notificational Nurture

Transactional messages — booking confirmations, reminders, follow-ups. Functional, not persuasive.

Lead Nurture

Emotional and booking-focused. The goal is to get them on a call. Use story, social proof, and urgency.
Personalisation through GHL logic branching — different paths based on responses, tags, and behaviour.
Custom values let you insert dynamic content (name, service interest, location) without building separate sequences.

Sales Nurture

Logical and sale-priming. The prospect is already booked or post-call. Use case studies, ROI breakdowns, and process walkthroughs to reduce pre-purchase anxiety.
Awareness spectrum positioning — tailor content to where they are: unaware → problem-aware → solution-aware → product-aware → most aware.

A/B Testing: GHL & Meta

Sophie / Ciara

Systematic testing across landing pages (GoHighLevel) and ad campaigns (Meta Experiments). The discipline is in knowing what to test, testing one variable at a time, and waiting for statistical significance.

GHL Landing Page Testing (Sophie)

What to test: headlines, VSLs, survey vs form, CTA copy, hero imagery, above-the-fold layout.
What NOT to test: minor colour changes, button shapes, font swaps — things that won't meaningfully move conversion rates.
50/50 traffic split — GHL handles this natively. Cookie-based assignment so the same visitor always sees the same variant.
Statistical significance: need 100+ data points and 2-4 weeks of runtime. Don't call a test early.

Meta A/B Testing (Ciara)

Campaign-level testing — run via Meta's Experiments page, not by duplicating ad sets manually. Meta handles the randomised audience segmentation.
One variable at a time — test creative OR audience OR placement, never multiple simultaneously. If you change headline + image, you won't know which moved the needle.
Minimum runtime: 30 days recommended. Let the algorithm fully learn before drawing conclusions.

Google Experiments

Raj

Google's built-in experimentation framework for A/B testing campaign changes before committing. Use the "Custom experiment" type for controlled, cookie-based testing.

Custom experiment type — select this over the other experiment options. It gives you the most control over what you're testing.
50/50 cookie-based split — Google assigns each user to a cohort consistently. A user in group A always sees variant A.
30-day minimum — run experiments for at least 30 days before making decisions. Shorter windows produce unreliable data.
Step-by-step: Create experiment → Set up variant campaign → Choose split percentage → Set schedule → Monitor → Apply winner (or end test).
Test one change at a time — same discipline as Meta testing. Isolate the variable or the result is meaningless.

Google Brand Campaigns

Sophie

A separate, low-budget Google Ads campaign specifically for your own brand name keywords. Defensive positioning — make sure you own the top spot when someone searches your name.

Exact + phrase match only — target your brand name and close variants. No broad match; you'd bleed into unrelated queries.
Low budget, high intent — brand searches already know you. The CPC is cheap and the conversion rate is typically the highest of any campaign.
Competitor ad groups — as a retaliation tactic. If competitors bid on your brand, create ad groups targeting their names. Use cautiously and only when warranted.
Decision flowchart: Are competitors bidding on your name? → Yes → Run brand campaign immediately. No → Still run it, but minimal budget as insurance.

Lead Quality Tracking

Patsy

Getting leads is meaningless if you can't trace which campaigns produce revenue. Track lead quality from first click to closed sale, then feed that data back into your ad strategy.

UTM parameters — tag every ad link with source, medium, campaign, and content UTMs. This lets you trace each lead back to the exact ad and audience that generated it.
Lead tracker spreadsheet — maintain a live tracker that maps each lead from ad click → form fill → booking → show → close. Identify where each stage leaks.
AI call transcript review — use AI tools to transcribe and analyse sales calls. Pattern-match what top-performing leads say versus low-quality ones, then refine ad copy to attract more of the former.
Stages after launch — don't just set and forget. Review lead quality weekly for the first 60 days, then biweekly. Adjust targeting and copy based on actual close rates, not just lead volume.

Organic Content Marketing

Raj

Organic content builds the brand that paid traffic converts. The distinction between business and brand matters: a business sells a service; a brand commands trust and repeat attention.

The 3 E's: Educate, Entertain, Engage. Every post should do at least one. The best posts do two.
Post frequency: 1-2 times per week is enough. Consistency matters more than volume.
Reuse organic as ads — posts that perform well organically already have social proof. Turn them into paid creatives with the engagement visible.
Brand vs performance marketing — performance (paid) drives immediate results; brand (organic) compounds over time. Both are necessary; neither replaces the other.
Channel adoption — don't spread across every platform. Pick 1-2 where your audience actually lives, and go deep.

Show Rates & Sales Reps

Brandon

Getting leads is only half the equation. If they don't show up to calls, nothing downstream works. And when they do show, the person on the other end of the call determines whether the system prints money.

Target 70% show rate — below that, your nurture sequence or booking process has a leak. Diagnose before scaling.
Book calls before lunch — morning calls have significantly higher show rates. Afternoon slots, especially late afternoon, see the worst no-show rates.
Hiring reps via Meta lead ads — run ads specifically for sales rep recruitment. Screen for coachability and hunger, not just experience.
Interview process: mock calls + ethics tests. Can they sell without being pushy? Do they handle objections with empathy? These matter more than a polished CV.
Scaling playbook: fill one rep's calendar → prove the conversion rate → hire the next rep. Never hire ahead of demand.
B2B timing insight: Decision-makers are freshest in the morning. Booking patterns should reflect when your prospect is most likely to be in "decide mode," not when your calendar has gaps.

Identity & Growth

Brandon

The closing summit session ties marketing strategy back to personal identity and business vision. The way you see yourself determines the ceiling of your business.

Identity shapes action — naive realism applies to self-perception too. If you see yourself as "a small business owner who does a bit of marketing," your campaigns will reflect that. Redefine the identity first.
US expansion opportunity — Where U? discussed growth into the US market, noting the scale difference and adaptation required. What works in Australia needs recalibration for US audiences.
Alumni perks & community — ongoing access to resources, advisory calls, and the member community. The summit is a starting point, not a one-off event.
Final takeaway: The best marketing system in the world won't work if the person running it doesn't believe they deserve the results it can produce. Fix the operator before you fix the funnel.
